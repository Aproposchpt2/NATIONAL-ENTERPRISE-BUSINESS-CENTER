From assessment to plan

Establish the baseline. Use the business assessment to understand the current stage and readiness gaps.
Choose the primary objective. Focus the plan on the most important business outcome rather than trying to solve every issue at once.
Define measurable actions. Convert broad goals into specific tasks, documents, milestones and decisions.
Review and adjust. Treat the plan as an operating tool that changes as the business completes milestones and gains new information.

Business planning FAQ

Do I need a full business plan before starting?

Not always. The appropriate planning depth depends on the business stage and objective. A focused action plan may be more useful for an operating business with a specific development need, while financing or institutional requirements may call for a more formal plan.

Should I complete the NEBC assessment first?

For first-time users, yes. NEBC's intended onboarding path uses the assessment to establish a baseline before post-assessment guidance and deeper business-development work.

Can planning include government contracting?

Contract readiness can be part of business planning. Actual state and local opportunity discovery is handled through the specialized NAT-CORP procurement pathway.
